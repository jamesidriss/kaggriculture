Generated by `benchmark/analyze_top2.py`. Every number is recomputed
from 1984 per-game rows in `experiments/top2/v51_vs_farm_*.csv`.

Design: **paired**. 1,000 fresh ladder-derived elite worlds (both players
rated >= 2900 in the public replay index), every world played from BOTH
seats. Worlds are disjoint from `REAL_dev` / `REAL_holdout` / `REAL_final`,
so nothing here was used to select anything.

## 4. Cash margin, not just the win

Win rate is the promotion metric, but the economic question is separate:
does A end richer, or merely more often ahead?

- mean paired margin: **$28**
- paired bootstrap 95% CI: **[$-63, $118]**
- median paired margin: **$53**
- games decided by under **$500**: 716/1984 (36.1%)
- games decided by under **$1,000**: 1170/1984 (59.0%)
- games decided by under **$2,000**: 1671/1984 (84.2%)
- games decided by under **$5,000**: 1927/1984 (97.1%)

The margins are small relative to the bank. Several thousand games were
needed to move the win-rate interval, which is itself the finding: these
two agents are near-indistinguishable on ladder-derived worlds.

## 5. World-regime split

- **elite_3000+** (146 worlds): A 149-141, 0.5138, Wilson [0.4565, 0.5708]  (not separated)
- **strong_2900_2999** (854 worlds): A 890-804, 0.5254, Wilson [0.5016, 0.5491]  (separated)

v51 is ahead in both bands. The higher-rated `elite_3000+` band is the smaller sample and is resolved on its own; the bulk of the evidence is the `strong_2900_2999` band. There is no evidence that v51 dominates one regime and Farm dominates another, so the 'complementary hedge' reading is NOT supported by these data.

## 6. Close-game structure

- worlds where the outcome is **the same from both seats**: 961/992 (96.9%)
- worlds where the winner **flips with the seat**: 31/992 (3.1%)

A **3.1%** flip rate is the important structural fact, and it cuts
against the easy story. A world almost always produces the same winner
from either seat, so the outcome is a property of the world rather than of
seat order or run-to-run noise. The two agents are therefore not separated
by world variance; they are separated by about two percentage points of
genuine skill, and it took 1,984 paired games to establish that at all.

The flip rate also bounds how much better a bigger sample could do: it
would sharpen the interval, but the underlying gap is small by
construction, not by sampling error.

## 7. What this does and does not establish

ESTABLISHED:
- v51 beats Farm **1039-945** over **1984** paired ladder worlds, 52.37%, Wilson [50.2%, 54.6%], exact binomial p = 0.0368. The interval excludes 50%, so the superiority is real.
- The margin is economically negligible: mean paired margin $28, bootstrap CI [$-63, $118] on banks of ~$100,000. 59% of games are decided by under $1,000.
- No seat advantage for either agent (McNemar p = 0.901).
- Only 3.1% of worlds flip winner with the seat.

NOT ESTABLISHED:
- That the advantage is commercially meaningful. 52.4% on razor-thin
  margins is a real but very small edge, and it is dwarfed by world choice.
- Anything about Kaggle ratings. Offline win rate is not a rating, and a
  validation score is not a ladder score. Three different quantities, never
  interchanged here.
