## Step 1 — column semantics, established empirically

Both of these were wrong in an earlier analysis, and both wrongnesses produced a published conclusion that was the opposite of the truth.

| column | what it actually is | how that was established |
|---|---|---|
| `reward_0/1` | **final cash**, not a win flag | values reach ~100,000; the two columns' means differ by under 0.1% |
| `score_0/1` | the two players' ladder ratings | balanced: P(score_0 > score_1) = 0.4995 |

`reward_*` read as an outcome makes `P(higher rated wins)` come out at ~0.00, which reads as "the ladder is pure noise". Read correctly it is one of the strongest signals available. The first version of `ladder_informativeness.py` made exactly that error and is preserved in the module docstring alongside the correction.

A rating column that is POST-game, not pre-match, is recorded as `rating_semantics='post_crawl'` in the lake so no downstream model can mistake it.

## Step 3 — what this says about the objective

The curve saturates near 1.0 by a 200-point gap. So:

- a 130-point advantage over the field wins **~96%** of games;
- a 200-point advantage wins **~99.5%**.

**Reaching 3075 is therefore not 'beat the field more often'. It is 'be effectively untouchable'.** That reframes the target: the gate is a win rate in the high nineties against a strong independent field, not a small edge.

It also explains why this project's league cannot be calibrated: the champion already wins essentially every matchup, which is the top of the curve.

## Step 5 — why plain Bradley-Terry was also withheld

BT is the textbook model and it was tried. The comparison graph here is a two-hub star whose bottom tier wins nothing, so the MLE is unbounded and any printed beta is an artefact of where the iteration stopped. Earlier work established this and published a REGULARIZED BT fit, correctly labelled. A point rating built on it would still be a point rating built on a model whose scale is arbitrary.

## What is published instead

Two things, both directly measured:

- **farm_2945_original** over **ahmedberatozer-v51-lean-flock** at 0.4763 over 3968 games → **+0 ladder points**, Wilson [0.4608, 0.4919]
- **ahmedberatozer-v51-lean-flock** over **ahmedberatozer-v49-funded-sale-timing** at 0.9464 over 392 games → **+109 ladder points**, Wilson [0.9195, 0.9647]

Everything else is a bound, not a point. Per-head-to-head Wilson intervals remain the primary evidence, and the promotion gate uses direct paired win rate rather than any rating.

## What would make this adequate

One thing: **a mid-strength agent whose real Kaggle `publicScore` is public and whose artifact is reusable.** Measured against such an agent, the win rate would land mid-curve, where it is not censored, and the whole graph would un-censor at once.

The available anchors are not that:

- `sunrise_v1..v5` have genuine publicScores (139.0–348.1) but lose essentially every game, so they sit far past the saturated end;
- `farm_2945_original` = 2945 and `barnyard_v7` = 3034.8 are **self-reported notebook titles, not ladder scores**, and are treated as weak anchors only.
